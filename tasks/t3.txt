Task , Collect Historical Pricing : 


My View on task . **Historical pricing is actually one of the more feasible parts of this project**, because GIDC has preserved an official archive of allotment-price revisions.

I checked the GIDC archive, and this changes the approach I would recommend.

### 1. Start with GIDC's historical allotment-price archive

GIDC currently provides historical allotment-price documents going back to **FY 2000–01**, including annual records for 2000–01 through 2023–24/2024–25. ([GIDC][1])

So rather than trying to reconstruct history from property websites, we can build:

```text
GIDC Estate
     ↓
2000-01 price
2001-02 price
2002-03 price
...
2025-26 price
2026-27 price
```

This gives us a **formal price time series**.

For example, GIDC's 2004 circular states that the revised allotment prices became effective from **1 April 2004** and gives estate-specific prices. ([GIDC][2])

The 2016 circular similarly provides estate-level allotment prices effective from **1 April 2016**. ([GIDC][3])

---

## 2. But "since inception" requires a second layer

This is the important distinction.

Suppose:

> Sanand Industrial Estate was established in year X.

The GIDC archive may give us prices from 2000 onward, but the estate may have existed before 2000.

Therefore:

```text
Estate inception
       ↓
Original allotment price
       ↓
Price revisions
       ↓
2000
       ↓
2001
       ↓
...
2026
```

For the period **before 2000**, we need to search additional historical records.

I'd use:

### A. GIDC circulars

Search for:

```text
GIDC allotment price 1980
GIDC allotment price 1985
GIDC allotment price 1990
GIDC allotment price 1995
```

and variations such as:

```text
"allotment price" GIDC
"land price" GIDC
"industrial estate" "price"
"GIDC" "Rs. per sq. metre"
```

### B. GIDC annual reports

These can establish:

* when an estate was created
* land acquired
* plots developed
* plots allotted
* investment
* historical policies

GIDC maintains an annual-report/account section, although the currently exposed online archive is much less complete than the allotment-price archive. ([GIDC][4])

### C. Old government resolutions

Search Gujarat Government archives for:

```text
GIDC
industrial estate
land allotment
land price
development charge
allotment rate
Gujarat Industrial Development Corporation
```

### D. Old GIDC circulars

These are particularly valuable because they can tell us:

> "Effective from 01-04-1995, allotment price for Estate X = ₹___ per sq.m."

That is much stronger evidence than a real-estate website saying:

> "Land prices were approximately ₹___ in 1995."

---

# 3. We should NOT call all historical prices the same thing

This is critical for your database.

You should have:

```text
price_type
```

with values such as:

```text
ORIGINAL_ALLOTMENT_PRICE
REVISED_ALLOTMENT_PRICE
TRANSFER_PRICE
AUCTION_PRICE
MARKET_PRICE
RESALE_PRICE
```

Because these are economically different.

For example, GIDC says its **allotment** activity is governed by its land/property disposal regulations, and its inventory is periodically updated. It also distinguishes saturated estates where new-industry allotment can occur through public auction. ([GIDC][5])

So:

**₹4,000/sq.m GIDC allotment price**

is not necessarily equivalent to:

**₹8,000/sq.m auction price**

and neither is necessarily equivalent to:

**₹12,000/sq.m private-market resale price.**

---

# 4. I would create a dedicated historical price table

Something like:

```text
industrial_land_price_history
```

| Field           | Example       |
| --------------- | ------------- |
| estate_id       | SANAND-01     |
| estate_name     | Sanand        |
| price_type      | ALLOTMENT     |
| effective_from  | 2022-04-01    |
| effective_to    | 2023-03-31    |
| price_per_sqm   | 4160          |
| currency        | INR           |
| sector          | Industrial    |
| category        | General       |
| source_document | GIDC circular |
| source_url      | ...           |
| source_date     | ...           |
| collection_date | ...           |
| confidence      | HIGH          |

This is much better than simply having:

```text
estate.current_price
```

# 5. Then calculate the price history

Once you have the data:

```text
Sanand

2000     ₹X
2001     ₹X
2002     ₹X
...
2015     ₹X
2016     ₹4,160
...
2022     ₹4,160
...
2026     ₹X
```

You can calculate:

### Absolute change

```text
Price(t) - Price(t-1)
```

### Percentage change

```text
[Price(t) - Price(t-1)] / Price(t-1) × 100
```

### CAGR

For an estate from 2005 to 2026:

```text
CAGR =
(Price_2026 / Price_2005)^(1/21) - 1
```

But I would **not automatically interpret this as land-market appreciation**.

It is the growth of the **official GIDC allotment price**, which is a different variable.

# 6. Even better: capture the reason for price revision

This is where the dataset becomes genuinely interesting.

Don't just store:

```text
2016 → ₹3,600
2021 → ₹5,xxx
2026 → ₹6,xxx
```

Also capture:

```text
revision_reason
```

Possible values:

```text
Infrastructure development
Land acquisition cost
Development expenditure
Market review
Administrative revision
Estate saturation
New phase
MSME policy
Special category
Unknown
```

The 2004 GIDC circular, for instance, explicitly discusses infrastructure conditions associated with the allotment prices. ([GIDC][2])

# 7. We can reconstruct an estate's entire price history

Imagine we select **Sanand**.

Our process would be:

```text
Sanand
  │
  ├── Estate inception date
  │
  ├── Original GIDC document
  │
  ├── 1980s records
  │
  ├── 1990s records
  │
  ├── 2000-01
  ├── 2001-02
  ├── 2002-03
  │       ...
  ├── 2015-16
  ├── 2016-17
  │       ...
  ├── 2021-22
  ├── 2022-23
  ├── 2023-24
  ├── 2024-25
  ├── 2025-26
  └── 2026-27
```

GIDC also has estate-specific material; for example, a Sanand estate document reports a **2022–23 base allotment price of ₹4,160/sq.m**. ([GIDC][6])

# 8. There is one major problem we need to solve

**Estate names change.**

For example:

```text
Sanand
Sanand RIDC
Sanand-II
Sanand-II (Bol)
Sanand MSME Park
```

may represent different estates/phases rather than simply different names for the same estate.

Therefore we need an **estate identity table**:

```text
estate_master
```

```text
estate_id
canonical_name
historical_name
district
taluka
inception_date
phase
parent_estate
former_name
gidc_code
```

Then historical documents can be mapped to the correct entity.

---

# 9. This is where LLM can help—but shouldn't be the authority

I would use an LLM to extract historical records from documents:

```text
PDF
 ↓
OCR
 ↓
LLM extraction
 ↓
Structured JSON
 ↓
Validation
 ↓
Human/Rule verification
 ↓
Database
```

For example:

```json
{
  "estate": "Sanand",
  "effective_date": "2004-04-01",
  "price": 1234,
  "unit": "INR/sqm",
  "price_type": "ALLOTMENT",
  "source": "GIDC Circular",
  "confidence": "HIGH"
}
```

But the LLM **does not decide whether the historical price is correct**.

The source document remains the authority.

---

# 10. I would make "historical reconstruction" a separate pipeline

Your overall architecture then becomes:

```text
                    GIDC
                     │
          ┌──────────┴──────────┐
          ↓                     ↓
    Current Data          Historical Archive
          │                     │
          │              2000 → 2026
          │                     │
          │              Pre-2000 Search
          │                     │
          └──────────┬──────────┘
                     ↓
              Estate Resolution
                     ↓
              Price Normalization
                     ↓
              Historical Database
                     ↓
             Price Time Series
```

And ultimately you can query:

> **"Show me the historical GIDC allotment-price trajectory of every Gujarat industrial estate since inception."**

That is a substantially more valuable dataset than merely collecting today's plot prices.

**And the good news is that we don't have to start from scratch:** GIDC's official archive gives us a fairly strong **2000–2026 backbone** already. ([GIDC][1])

The next step I would recommend is to **test this methodology on 3 Gujarat parks—e.g. Sanand, Vatva and Naroda—and reconstruct their price history year-by-year, including the original source document for every price point.** That would tell us how much of the "since inception" history can actually be recovered before designing the full India-wide pipeline.
